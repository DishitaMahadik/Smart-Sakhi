Everyday habits that keep you safe

A few small habits, practised by every member, protect the whole group. Share these at your next meeting.

Do this

  • Lock your phone with a PIN or pattern, and lock the SIM card too
  • Install apps only from Google Play Store
  • Pause and call a trusted family member before paying anyone new
  • Update your apps and phone software when reminded
  • Tell your group leader if you receive a suspicious message

Never do this

  • Share your OTP, PIN, or password — with anyone, even "bank staff"
  • Click links in messages promising prizes, refunds, or free gifts
  • Install an app just because someone on a call asked you to
  • Save your PIN or passwords as notes or contacts in your phone
  • Send money to "confirm" your identity or unlock a prize

Frauds that target women's groups the most

These five tricks come up again and again in SHG meetings. Learn the pattern, and you'll spot the next one instantly.

The OTP call

A caller claims to be from your bank and says your account will be blocked unless you share the OTP right now. Real banks never ask for your OTP over a call.

The instant loan app

An app promises quick loans with no paperwork, then charges hidden fees and threatens borrowers using contacts copied from their phone.

The KYC update message

A text says your bank or SHG account will be frozen unless you "update KYC" through a link. The link installs a fake app that reads your messages.

The lucky winner message

"You have won ₹50,000!" — but first you must pay a small "processing fee". Nobody wins a lottery they never entered.

The government scheme link

A forwarded message about a "new government scheme for women" asks you to register with your Aadhaar and bank details on an unofficial site.

The hacked friend's message

A message from a friend's number asks to borrow money urgently. Their WhatsApp may be hacked — call them on a different number to confirm first.
